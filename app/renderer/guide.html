TTimotheos – User Guide

Timotheos

An offline Bible study program for Windows and Linux that reads theWord and e-Sword modules. This guide explains the program on a computer; the phone app has a guide of its own. Press F1 in Timotheos to open it again.

Getting started

  1. Install Timotheos with its installer (Windows) or its package (Linux) and start it. On Windows, SmartScreen may warn the first time because the program is not code-signed: choose More info → Run anyway.
  2. Timotheos comes with one Bible, Ang Malayang Biblia (New Testament). Bring in your own modules in either of two ways:
    • Import (toolbar, File → Import Modules…, Ctrl+I), or drag module files onto the window. Timotheos checks each file and copies the ones it can use into its own library. A report then says, for every file, what it was recognised as, or why it was not imported. .zip and theWord .twzip files are unpacked.
    • Options → Modules → Add folder… (Ctrl+O) reads modules where they already are, for example theWord's or e-Sword's own module folder, without copying them. Sub-folders are searched too.
  3. Each Bible is read in a Bible view of its own. Click the Bible's name at the top of a view to choose another Bible; Panels → Bible View opens one more view.

The screen

  • Title bar: the File, View and Help menus (Alt+F, Alt+V, Alt+H). It takes the colours of your skin.
  • Toolbar, from left to right:
    • Import.
    • Panels: show or hide panels, and choose a layout.
    • Skin.
    • Options.
    Words beside the icons can be hidden in Options → Layout & toolbar.
  • Bible view in the middle, with the book and chapter in its caption and A− / A+ for the text size.
  • Panels around it: Books & chapters, Commentary, Library, Bible search, Book search, My Study.
  • Status bar at the bottom: the current verse and how many modules are loaded. It can be turned off in Options.

Moving and arranging panels

  • Drag a panel by its caption or its tab. While you drag, arrow targets appear:
    • The three at the window edges dock the panel on the left, right or bottom.
    • Over another panel, a small compass lets you put it above/below (or left/right of) that panel, or + adds it as a tab there.
    • A shaded box shows where it will go.
  • Drop a panel anywhere else to float it as a small window. Move a floating window by its caption and resize it from its bottom-right corner.
  • Caption buttons:
    • A− / A+: text size, on panels with Bible, commentary, dictionary or book text.
    • Float / dock again.
    • Maximize.
    • Close.
    Double-click a caption to float it or dock it again.
  • The window is a grid of 4 columns × 3 rows: 12 areas. Every kind of panel, Bible views included, can be dragged by its caption to any of the 12 areas (drop it on an area to put it there, or on an occupied area to make it a tab). Drop it outside the grid to float it.
  • Drag the narrow gaps between the areas to make columns wider and rows taller. Size of everything in this panel in a panel's palette menu makes its fonts and controls smaller or larger.
  • Panels on the toolbar:
    • Bible View adds another Bible view, already showing a Bible. Closed panels are reopened here too.
    • Offers ready layouts: Classic, Compact, Word study, Reading.
    • Save current layout as… keeps your own arrangement under a name.
  • Reading focus (F11, or View → Reading Focus) hides the docked panels; press it again to bring them back.
  • Your layout is remembered when you close Timotheos. View → Reset Panel Layout goes back to the classic layout.

Reading the Bible

Going to a passage

  • Press Ctrl+G, or click the verse reference at the top of a Bible view, and choose the book, chapter and verse. You can also type a reference in the box at the top of the Books & chapters panel, for example Jn 3:16, Juan 3:16, Tob 5, Ecclus 24:3, and press Enter.
  • Or use the book and chapter lists, or click a chapter in the Books & chapters panel.
  • Keys in the Bible text:
    • ← →: chapter.
    • Ctrl+← Ctrl+→: book.
    • ↑ ↓: verse.
    • Type a number to jump to that verse (Shift+number: chapter).
    • Alt+← Alt+→ or Backspace: back and forward through the places you visited.
  • Click a verse number to select the verse. The Commentary panel follows the selected verse.

Bible views (several Bibles)

Each Bible is read in a Bible view of its own; the views stand side by side in the middle of the window.

  • Panels → Bible View opens another view; click × in its caption to close it. theWord and e-Sword Bibles can be mixed.
  • The active view (with the coloured border) is the one you last clicked: the toolbar, the arrow keys and the Commentary panel follow it.
  • Click the Bible's name in a view's caption to choose another Bible for that view.
  • The link button: views normally move together. Switch it off and that view reads on by itself (its caption then shows its own book and chapter); switch it on again and it rejoins the others.
  • A− / A+ in a view's caption make only that view's text smaller or larger (also Ctrl + mouse wheel over the view).
  • The side bar of a view switches things for that view only: S Strong's numbers, M morphology, F footnotes, H headings, J words of Jesus in red, I added words in italics, X cross-references, L lemmas, ⫼ intralinear view, # verse numbers (in the text or hidden), Aa the font of the view, 🔊 read aloud and ⚙ more options.
  • ⚙ in the side bar: footnote marks as 1 2 3, a b c or *, the footnotes listed under the chapter, which lines the intralinear view shows, and the Bible's own switches (some theWord Bibles define switches of their own, e.g. V for variant readings; a one-letter switch also works as a key).
  • Intralinear View (Alt+I) puts each tagged word over its original Greek or Hebrew word, a transliteration, the Strong's number, the morphology and the lemma. Where the Bible gives no lemma, the original word comes from your Strong's dictionary.
  • View → Side by side (Ctrl+H) or One under another (Ctrl+J) arranges the views.
  • Panels → Mark differences compares the first view with the others (those that move together) word by word.

Words, dictionaries and commentaries

  • Word popups: rest the mouse on a tagged word, a Strong's number or a morphology code. The popup shows the morphology first, then the dictionary entry for the Strong's number.
    • Drag the popup's bottom-right corner to make it bigger or smaller; Timotheos remembers the size.
    • Click the word to keep the entry in the Lookup panel.
    • Find G26 in an entry searches the open Bibles for that number.
  • Clicking an ordinary word looks it up in your dictionaries and books (similar word forms are marked ~).
  • Which dictionaries are used for Strong's numbers and morphology: Options → Modules → Dictionaries.
  • Commentary panel: the comments on the selected verse. Each commentary is a button.
    • A filled dot means it comments on this very verse.
    • A ring means it comments on the passage, chapter or book.
  • Library panel: open any commentary, dictionary, book or devotional.
    • Choose the module in the list at the top. Its topics are listed on the left and the text is shown on the right.
    • Drag the line between the topic list and the text to make the list wider or narrower (double-click the line to reset it). The first button on the bar hides or shows the list.
    • Type in Find a topic… to filter the list.
    • Arrow buttons on the bar:
      • Left/right arrows (or Alt+← / Alt+→): previous or next topic.
      • Back arrow: the topic you read before.
      • In the list, ↑ ↓, Page Up Page Down, Home End also work.
    • Links in the text work; Bible references jump to the verse.
    • Devotionals open at today's reading.
    • ⓘ opens the module's Properties; the box icon makes the file smaller (see below).
  • Cross-references: when X is on, a verse that has them ends with a small mark such as †3 (three references). Click the mark to list them. ≡ → …written out in the text prints the references themselves in the text instead.
    • References in [brackets] point to this verse.
    • Rest the mouse on one to read it; click it to go there.
    • Right-click a verse → Add a cross-reference… keeps your own references.

Your study: highlights, notes, bookmarks, lists, reading plans

  • Right-click a verse to:
    • Copy it, or open Copy verses….
    • Add it to the verse list.
    • Set a bookmark.
    • Highlight the selected words (or the whole verse) in five colours, or underline or bold them.
    • Write a note.
    • See or add cross-references.
    • Open its commentaries.
    • Search for the word or its Strong's number.
  • Notes: references you type in a note (e.g. Rom 5:8) become links. With N on, notes show under their verse.
  • Bookmarks: Ctrl+Shift+1 … 0 sets bookmark 1–0 at the current verse; Ctrl+1 … 0 jumps to it.
  • My Study panel:
    • Verse lists: named lists. Ctrl+L adds the current verse; you can reorder, sort and copy a list.
    • Notes: all your notes.
    • Bookmarks.
    • Reading: today's readings and devotionals, plus reading plans.
      • A plan can cover the whole Bible, the OT and NT together, the 1611 Apocrypha, or books you choose.
      • You can read every day or on chosen weekdays.
      • Finish in a number of days, by a date, or with so many chapters each time.
      • Tick readings off, catch up or restart.

Searching

Open the Bible search panel (Ctrl+F), type a query and press Enter. The line under the box says how Timotheos understood your query.

  • Range: the whole Bible; OT with or without the Apocrypha; NT; Law; Gospels; Paul; the current book or chapter; or your own (Gen-Deut; Matt 5-7, which can be saved).
  • Results:
    • Verses: with the found words marked.

    • + List adds all the found verses to your verse list; Copy copies them; ⟲ brings back earlier searches.

Book search

The Book search panel (Ctrl+Shift+F, beside the Bible search) looks for words in commentaries, dictionaries, books, devotionals and maps – not in the Bibles.

  • Every word must occur in a topic; "a phrase" must occur as written; creat* matches words that start with creat. Case and accents are ignored unless you tick Match accents; Whole words finds only whole words.
  • Choose where to look: all book modules, one kind (e.g. only the commentaries), one module, or a module set of your own (Sets…: tick the modules and give the set a name).
  • Results are grouped by module, with the words marked. Click one to open it in the Library, with the words marked there too.
  • Large libraries take a while: the line above the results says which module is being read.

Copying verses

  • Ctrl+C copies the current verse, or the text you selected.
  • Copy verses window (F5, the copy icon on a Bible view's icon bar, or right-click a verse):
    • Step 1: the From and To buttons open a Book → Chapter → Verse picker. You can type the first letters of a book to find it.
      • The mouse wheel over a button, or Page Up / Page Down, moves that end one verse. Hold Shift to move a chapter at a time.
      • A range can cross chapters and books.
      • The verse count shows next to the range; × goes back to one verse.
    • Step 2: choose the Bible and the format. The preview on the right changes as you go.
    • Drag the window's bottom-right corner to resize it; the size is remembered.
  • Templates (format Custom…):
    • %r range
    • %t Bible
    • %b book
    • %c chapter
    • %v verse
    • %T verse text
    • %n new line
    Plain text only drops italics and red letters. Your last format is used again next time and for Ctrl+C.

Text size

  • A− / A+ in the caption of each Bible view (only that view's text) and of the Commentary, Lookup, Library and search panels.
  • Ctrl + mouse wheel over any of those panels: one notch makes its text one step bigger or smaller. Each panel keeps its own size.
  • Ctrl + / Ctrl − change the text size of the active Bible view.
  • Options → Fonts sets the fonts, line spacing, text width, and the size of menus and panel text. One view can have its own line spacing: ☰ → Line spacing.

Modules Timotheos reads

Download modules inside Timotheos

File → Download Modules… lists modules you can fetch without leaving the program. Choose a repository at the top:

  • Timotheos: modules offered by the author of Timotheos.
  • eBible.org: Bibles in very many languages that their publishers allow to be shared. Type a language or a title to find one.
  • Add a repository…: paste the address of a catalog that another site publishes for Timotheos. Remove this one takes it out of the list again.

Click Download beside a module. Timotheos fetches it, imports it and says when it is in your library. In this window Timotheos uses the internet only when you ask. (The only other time is the look for a new version of Timotheos itself, see Check for updates.) Nothing about you or your study is sent.

Making a catalog. A catalog is a small text file (JSON) on a web site with an https address: { "name": "…", "about": "…", "modules": [ { "title": "…", "url": "https://…/module.ont", "language": "…", "kind": "Bible", "description": "…", "licence": "…", "size": 1234567 } ] }. Only title and url are needed. List only modules you have the right to share.

Where to get modules

File → Where to Get Modules… opens this list with links you can click:

  • wordmodules.com: theWord modules (Bibles, commentaries, dictionaries, books, devotionals).
  • biblesupport.com: e-Sword modules. Choose the e-Sword downloads, not MySword.
  • ebible.org: Bibles in very many languages. Download a translation as USFM (the _usfm.zip file) or USFX and import it as it is; Timotheos reads both. It has no theWord or e-Sword files.
  1. Download the module to your computer. Do not run it and do not unzip it.
  2. In Timotheos click Import (Ctrl+I) and choose the files, or drag them onto the window.
  3. The report says what was imported. Bibles appear in the list of Bibles of each view, commentaries in the Commentary panel, dictionaries and books in the Library.

A module that comes as a program (.exe) cannot be imported. A site may ask for a free account. Timotheos is not connected with these sites.

theWord books written with RichView (many user-made commentaries and books) are shown with their formatting: bold and italic, colours, centred paragraphs, links, pictures and tables. They are read only.

Encrypted or locked modules are not opened. This covers theWord .ontx/.otx/.ntx, locked .twm modules and encrypted e-Sword modules. Their licences forbid getting around that protection, so Timotheos names them and skips them.

Not supported yet: e-Sword harmonies, graphics and notes files, and theWord reading-plan modules.

Module properties

Open a module's properties with ⓘ on the Library bar, or with the small ⓘ that appears when you rest the mouse on a module in the Bibles or Modules panel.

  • About: the module's description shown as a formatted page. Edit lets you change it, with bold, italic, underline and lists. Timotheos saves it in the form the module's program reads: RTF for theWord and e-Sword 9/10, HTML for e-Sword 11.
  • Details: title, abbreviation, description, author, version, publisher and so on.
  • All settings: every other setting stored in the module. Settings that belong to the program that made the module are shown greyed out and are not changed.
  • File & size: where the file is and how big it is.
    • Make smaller compacts the file. This removes unused space; nothing in the module changes.
    • For theWord books you can also compress the text. Timotheos reads compressed books, but it has not yet been checked whether theWord reads books compressed by Timotheos, so test it in theWord first.

Before the first change, Timotheos keeps a copy of the original file next to it (…timotheos-backup). Put the original file back undoes all changes. Locked (encrypted) modules cannot be changed.

To remove an imported module, rest the mouse on it in the Modules panel and click ×. The copy goes to the Recycle Bin. Modules read from your own folders are never deleted.

Tools

  • Pictures in commentaries, dictionaries and books (RTF and RichView) are shown in the text. Click one to open the picture viewer: the mouse wheel zooms, drag to move, ← → for the other pictures of the entry, Esc closes.
  • Bible references in entries (“John 3:16”, “1 Cor. 13:4-7”, e-Sword's “Php_1:17”) are links; rest the mouse on one to read the verse, click to go there.
  • Your own marks in books: select words in a commentary, dictionary or book topic and a small bar appears – highlight in a colour, bold, underline, or ⌫ to remove. The module is not changed; the marks are kept in your study data and come back whenever the topic is shown.
  • Clipboard monitor (View → Clipboard Monitor): while it is on, copy text with Bible references in any program (an e-mail, a web page) and a small window lists the references with their text. Click one to go there; + List adds them all to your verse list; to front brings Timotheos forward when references are copied.
  • Export topics (the export button on the Library bar, or File → Export Topics…): the open topic or all topics of a module as a Word document (.docx), Rich Text (.rtf), a web page (.html) or plain text (.txt). Locked modules are never read, so they cannot be exported.
  • Print the open Library topic with the print button on the Library bar; Ctrl+P prints the Bible chapter.

Where your things are kept

On Windows, all in %APPDATA%\Timotheos (paste that into the Explorer address bar). Help → About Timotheos shows the folder on any computer.

  • modules\: the modules you imported (File → Show Imported Modules Folder).
  • my-study-data.json: your highlights, notes, bookmarks, verse lists and reading plans. A backup is kept for each of the last 7 days.
  • My cross-references.xrefs.twm: the cross-references you added, in theWord's format.
  • settings.json: options, skin, layout and folders.
Back up your study. File → Back Up My Study… saves your notes, highlights, bookmarks, verse lists and reading plans as one file. File → Restore My Study… reads such a file, on this computer or another one, or on the phone: it can add to what is there or replace it, and it first sets a copy of what is there aside.

Timotheos

An offline Bible study app for your phone that reads theWord and e-Sword modules. This guide is about the phone app. Open it again from Help → User guide.

Getting started

  1. Timotheos comes with one Bible, Ang Malayang Biblia (New Testament), so there is something to read at once. The first time, choose one of three screens; Timotheos then starts in that screen. You can change it later with Change screen (in More, in Help or in the command bar):
    • Quiet Reader: only the text and one floating capsule.
    • Clear Tabs: tabs at the bottom for Bible, Library, Search, Notes and More.
    • Study Desk: the text with drawers on the left and right and a dock at the bottom.
  2. Bring in your modules with Modules → Import: choose theWord or e-Sword files on your phone. Timotheos checks each file, keeps a copy of the ones it can use, and tells you why any file was not imported. .zip and .twzip files are unpacked.
  3. Tap the Bible's short name (for example KJV) to choose another Bible.

Touch

  • Swipe the text left or right: next or previous chapter.
  • Tap a word: a small pop-up with its dictionary entries. Drag it by its top bar to move it. Timotheos remembers where you put it. Throw it off the screen to close it. Dictionary opens the full entry, and Find searches for the word.
  • Hold a verse: its tools (copy, highlight, underline, read aloud, compare, cross-references, commentary, note, bookmark, add to a list and more).
  • Pinch with two fingers: the Bible or book text gets larger or smaller. The buttons and menus keep their size.
  • Popups go back the way they came. Drag a sheet from the bottom back down, a list that dropped from the top bar back up, a page from the right back to the right, and a drawer back to its side. You don't need the ✕. A list inside a popup scrolls first; drag again once it is at its end.
  • Swipe up from the bottom edge: the bottom bar (tab bar, capsule or dock) comes back after it hid while you read. A tap on the text brings it back too. The phone's own home gesture uses the lowest strip of the screen, so start just above it.
  • The phone's Back button or gesture closes the topmost popup, page or drawer first.

Quiet Reader

  • Tap the page to show the top strip: Back, Forward, the place, Search and More. It hides again by itself.
  • Tap the place (for example John 3) to go to a book and chapter. Tap the Bible's short name beside it: the list of your Bibles drops down from under the strip, next to the name.
  • The big title at the top of the chapter works the same way. Its Aa button opens the reading view settings.
  • The capsule at the bottom: previous and next chapter, the reference (tap it for Book → Chapter → Verse) and read aloud. While a chapter is read aloud, it becomes a player.
  • More: a search box for verses, books and commands, then Library, Bible search, Book search, Commentary, My Study, Parallel views, Copy verses, Daily readings, Read aloud, Modules, Options and Help.

Clear Tabs

  • The top bar: Back, Forward, Aa (reading view) and read aloud.
  • Under it: the place (tap it for the list of books and chapters), and the Bible chip (tap it for the list of your Bibles). Two Bibles at once is in Options → Layout → Parallel Bibles and under More.
  • Tap a verse: a small toolbar to copy, highlight, underline or write a note. More adds read aloud, compare, cross-references, commentary, dictionary and bookmark. Hold a verse for all its tools.
  • The tabs: Bible, Library, Search, Notes and More. More holds read aloud, appearance, layout, verse numbers, reading view, modules, copy verses, clipboard monitor, daily readings, fonts, all options, the guide and About.

Study Desk

  • The rail at the top: Back, the reference (it opens the Navigator), the Bible's short name (the list of your Bibles drops from under the rail), Aa and Forward.
  • From the left edge: the Navigator, with books and chapters, history and bookmarks. From the right edge: the study drawer, with commentary, dictionary, cross-references, note and library, following the verse. Drag a drawer back to its edge to close it.
  • Pull down from the top, or tap the small handle under the rail: quick settings. Push them back up to close them.
  • The dock: Books & chapters, the command bar, read aloud and the study drawer. Pull up from the dock: the split desk with Parallel, Commentary, Library and Search.
  • Command bar: type a reference, a book, a word or a command.
  • Hold a verse: a quick row (copy, underline, note, read aloud, three highlight colours, more).

Reading

  • Two Bibles (Parallel): one above the other, with a divider you can drag. Each one has its own bar with its name, its place and its own Aa.
  • Reading view (Aa): the Bible, text size, margins, line height and text alignment (left, centre, justified, right).
    • Show in the text: Strong's numbers, morphology, interlinear, footnotes, headings, words of Jesus, added words, cross-references, commentary marks, your highlights and notes, and the module's own tags (for checking a module).
    • This view: font, heading font, text colour, active verse colour, the highlighter and underline pens, moving with the other view, verse numbers and About this Bible.
  • The text flows in paragraphs. A new line or paragraph starts only where the Bible module puts one.
  • Read aloud reads from the selected verse and goes on into the next chapter. Its settings choose a voice for each language, the speed, and whether to repeat a chapter or a range of verses. The voices are the ones your phone has.
  • The Apocrypha sits between Malachi and Matthew, as in the 1611 King James Bible. Options → Modules → Place the Apocrypha moves it after Revelation.

Library, search and study

  • Library: your commentaries, dictionaries, books, devotionals and maps. Devotionals open at today's reading.
  • Search: the Bibles, or every library book. Some examples:
    • faith hope: both words.
    • "in the beginning": the exact phrase.
    • love*: words that start with love.
    • G26 or H430: a Strong's number.
  • Study: your notes, highlights, bookmarks, verse lists and reading plans. Back up my study saves them all as one file where you choose (for example Downloads or Drive); Restore reads such a file, also one made on a computer.
  • Cross-references show as a small mark such as †3 at the end of a verse; tap it to list them. Aa → Cross-refs written out prints them in the text.
  • Copy verses: pick the range and the format. Ranges can run across chapters and books.
  • Your own marks in books: select words in a commentary or book and choose a colour, bold or underline. The module itself is not changed.

Modules

A verse in a dictionary entry

Tap a verse reference inside a word pop-up or a dictionary entry and the pop-up closes and the Bible shows that verse, selected. The Bible is your default Old or New Testament Bible (Options → Modules); when that is on The first open Bible, it is the first Bible in the list of the Bible view's name menu that has the book. If no Bible has it, Timotheos shows where to get Bibles.

Download modules inside Timotheos

Modules → Download modules lists modules you can fetch without leaving the app. Choose a repository at the top: Timotheos (from the author), eBible.org (Bibles in very many languages; type a language or a title to find one), or one you added with Add a repository….

Tap Download beside a module. Timotheos fetches it, imports it and says when it is in your library. A whole Bible is a few megabytes. In this window Timotheos uses the internet only when you ask. (The only other time is the look for a new version of Timotheos itself.)

Where to get modules

Modules → Where to get modules opens this list with links you can tap:

  • wordmodules.com: theWord modules.
  • biblesupport.com: e-Sword modules. Choose the e-Sword downloads, not MySword.
  • ebible.org: Bibles in very many languages. Download a translation as USFM (_usfm.zip) or USFX and import it as it is.

  2. In Timotheos: Modules → Import modules, and choose the files in Downloads.
  3. Timotheos keeps its own copy and tells you what it imported.
