TTimotheos – User Guide

Timotheos

An offline Bible study program for Windows and Linux that reads theWord and e-Sword modules. This guide explains the program on a computer; the phone app has a guide of its own. Press F1 in Timotheos to open it again.

Getting started

  1. Install Timotheos with its installer (Windows) or its package (Linux: a .deb, or the .AppImage) and start it. On Windows, SmartScreen may warn the first time because the program is not code-signed: choose More info → Run anyway.
  2. Timotheos comes with one Bible, Ang Malayang Biblia (New Testament). Bring in your own modules in either of two ways:
    • Import (toolbar, File → Import Modules…, Ctrl+I), or drag module files onto the window. Timotheos checks each file and copies the ones it can use into its own library. A report then says, for every file, what it was recognised as, or why it was not imported. .zip and theWord .twzip files are unpacked.
    • Options → Modules → Add folder… (Ctrl+O) reads modules where they already are, for example theWord's or e-Sword's own module folder, without copying them. Sub-folders across nested levels (including OS links, Windows .lnk shortcuts and .timotheos-link folder links) are searched too, and sub-folders or links whose name or target starts with - or . are skipped (theWord's convention for disabled folders).
  3. Each Bible is read in a Bible view of its own. Click the Bible's name at the top of a view to choose another Bible; Panels → Bible View opens one more view.

The screen

  • Title bar: the File, View and Help menus (Alt+F, Alt+V, Alt+H). It takes the colours of your skin.
  • Toolbar, from left to right:
    • Import.
    • Panels: show or hide panels, and choose a layout.
    • Skin.
    • Options.
    Words beside the icons can be hidden in Options → Layout & toolbar.
  • Bible view in the middle, with the book and chapter in its caption and A− / A+ for the text size.
  • Panels around it: Books & chapters, Commentary, Library, Bible search, Book search, My Study.
  • Status bar at the bottom: the current verse and how many modules are loaded. It can be turned off in Options.

Moving and arranging panels

  • Drag a panel by its caption or its tab. While you drag, arrow targets appear:
    • The three at the window edges dock the panel on the left, right or bottom.
    • Over another panel, a small compass lets you put it above/below (or left/right of) that panel, or + adds it as a tab there.
    • A shaded box shows where it will go.
  • Drop a panel anywhere else to float it as a small window. Move a floating window by its caption and resize it from its bottom-right corner.
  • Caption buttons:
    • A− / A+: text size, on panels with Bible, commentary, dictionary or book text.
    • Float / dock again.
    • Maximize.
    • Close.
    Double-click a caption to float it or dock it again.
  • Adding and moving panels (improved 0.6.95): open the Panels menu, then click, hold, and drag any panel kind (Bible View, Books & chapters, Commentary, Library, Bible search, Book search, or My Study) onto an available empty space at least 1″ × 1″ in size. The menu stays on top while you drag and available empty spaces highlight with a green border. Only the dropped panel sizes itself into that space; other panels stay in place without moving or splitting. If no empty space is available yet, the menu shows a notice reminding you to enable Detach edges and move or resize panels to open space.
  • Detach edges (0.6.82): switch on Detach edges in the toolbar (or Panels menu / Options → Layout & toolbar) while arranging to pull any panel edge inward without stretching its neighbour—opening empty spaces between or around panels—and to keep a panel's old space open when you move or close it. Switch it off when done to return to linked dividers and automatic gap-filling.
  • Drag the narrow gaps between the areas to make columns wider and rows taller. Size of everything in this panel in a panel's palette menu makes its fonts and controls smaller or larger.
  • Panels on the toolbar:
    • Bible View adds another Bible view, already showing a Bible. Closed panels are reopened here too.
    • Offers ready layouts (Classic, Compact, Word study, Reading) and your saved layouts, each shown with a live miniature thumbnail of its actual panel arrangement.
    • Save current layout as… keeps your own arrangement under a name.
  • Reading focus (F11, or View → Reading Focus) hides the docked panels; press it again to bring them back.
  • Your layout is remembered when you close Timotheos. View → Reset Panel Layout goes back to the classic layout.

Reading the Bible

Going to a passage

  • Press Ctrl+G, or click the verse reference at the top of a Bible view, and choose the book, chapter and verse. You can also type a reference in the box at the top of the Books & chapters panel, for example Jn 3:16, Juan 3:16, Tob 5, Ecclus 24:3, and press Enter.
  • Or use the book and chapter lists, or click a chapter in the Books & chapters panel.
  • Keys in the Bible text:
    • ← →: chapter.
    • Ctrl+← Ctrl+→: book.
    • ↑ ↓: verse.
    • Type a number to jump to that verse (Shift+number: chapter).
    • Alt+← Alt+→ or Backspace: back and forward through the places you visited.
  • Click a verse number to select the verse. The Commentary panel follows the selected verse.

Bible views (several Bibles)

Each Bible is read in a Bible view of its own; the views stand side by side in the middle of the window.

  • Panels → Bible View opens another view; click × in its caption to close it. theWord and e-Sword Bibles can be mixed.
  • The active view (with the coloured border) is the one you last clicked: the toolbar, the arrow keys and the Commentary panel follow it.
  • Click the Bible's name in a view's caption to choose another Bible for that view.
  • The link button: views normally move together. Switch it off and that view reads on by itself (its caption then shows its own book and chapter); switch it on again and it rejoins the others.
  • A− / A+ in a view's caption make only that view's text smaller or larger (also Ctrl + mouse wheel over the view).
  • The side bar of a view switches things for that view only: S Strong's numbers, M morphology, F footnotes, H headings, J words of Jesus in red, I added words in italics, X cross-references, L lemmas, ⫼ intralinear view, # verse numbers (in the text or hidden), Aa the font of the view, 🔊 read aloud and ⚙ more options.
  • ⚙ in the side bar: footnote marks as 1 2 3, a b c or *, the footnotes listed under the chapter, which lines the intralinear view shows, and the Bible's own switches (some theWord Bibles define switches of their own, e.g. V for variant readings; a one-letter switch also works as a key).
  • Intralinear View (Alt+I) puts each tagged word over its original Greek or Hebrew word, a transliteration, the Strong's number, the morphology and the lemma. Where the Bible gives no lemma, the original word comes from your Strong's dictionary. In an interlinear Bible the English gloss (the English word or phrase after each Greek or Hebrew word) is one of the lines, so it lines up with the codes. It is also shown raised, small and coloured when Intralinear View is off.
  • View → Side by side (Ctrl+H) or One under another (Ctrl+J) arranges the views.
  • Panels → Mark differences compares the first view with the others (those that move together) word by word.

Making a catalog. A catalog is a small text file (JSON) on a web site with an https address: { "name": "…", "about": "…", "modules": [ { "title": "…", "url": "https://…/module.ont", "language": "…", "kind": "Bible", "description": "…", "licence": "…", "size": 1234567 } ] }. Only title and url are needed. List only modules you have the right to share.

Archives and compressed files

Modules often come packed. Timotheos opens these directly, so you do not need to unpack anything first:

  • Archives: .zip, .twzip, .7z, .rar, .tar, and tar files compressed with gzip, bzip2, xz or zstd (.tar.gz or .tgz, .tar.bz2, .tar.xz, .tar.zst).
  • One compressed file: .gz, .bz2, .xz, .zst (for example MyBible.bbl.mybible.gz).
  • Timotheos looks at what is inside the file, not only at its name, so a file with the wrong ending still opens if it really is an archive.
  1. Use File → Import Modules… (Ctrl+I) and choose the archive (the file chooser has an Archives and compressed files line), or drag the archive onto the window.
  2. Timotheos opens it, finds the theWord, e-Sword, MySword, USFM and USFX modules inside (an archive inside an archive is opened too, up to 3 levels), and imports them. The results window lists what was added and what was skipped, and why.
  3. Protected archives: a box Password needed appears. Type the password and click Open. If it is wrong the box comes back (3 tries); Skip this archive leaves it out. The password is used once and is not saved.
  4. Archives in a modules folder (0.6.76, improved 0.6.81): an archive that you leave in one of your modules folders — or in any sub-folder inside it — is unpacked by itself a few seconds after Timotheos starts (and immediately when you add a folder or click Rescan), and a short message tells you how many modules it added. Sub-folders and links whose name or target starts with - or . are skipped. Clicking Rescan (or removing and re-adding the folder) re-unpacks any module archive whose unpacked file is missing, while failed unpacks are retried automatically. A protected archive is not opened this way: import it with Import to type its password.
  5. Your archive is never changed or deleted: Timotheos copies the modules out of it.

Study backup. File → Restore My Study… also accepts a backup that you compressed (for example .zip or .gz): it uses the .json backup inside.

Limits. An archive may unpack to at most about 800 MB. Standard .zip and .twzip archives stream straight to disk with minimal memory so large dictionaries import cleanly; other formats (such as .7z or .rar) unpack in memory and can be slower when very large. RAR files are read only (Timotheos cannot make them). Archives that contain no module Timotheos can read are listed as Not imported.

Rearranged Bibles (.order file)

iBiblia can rearrange the books, chapters and verses of a Bible and export it with a small companion file named like the module, ending in .order. Choose both files in File > Import Modules (or drop them on the window): Timotheos copies the order file next to the module and the Bible view then follows that order, including the book list and the previous/next chapter buttons. The verse numbers are not changed, only where they appear. Importing a newer module without its order file removes the old arrangement. Programs that do not know the file, such as theWord, simply show the normal order. Searches and links still go by the original reference, so they may not land on a moved verse.

Importing documents (.txt .rtf .docx .odt)

Use File → Import Modules… (Ctrl+I) or drag the file onto the window, exactly as for a module. When the file is a document, Timotheos asks what it should become:

  • Library book. The document is split into sections by its headings (Heading 1, Heading 2 … in Word or LibreOffice; in a .txt or .rtf, lines that look like titles: Chapter 1, an ALL-CAPS line, a # line, or a short bold or big line). Sections nest by heading level. A document with no headings becomes one section. Bold, italic, underline, paragraphs, lists, tables and pictures are kept. You find it in the Library under its name and can search it like any book. It is saved as a theWord book (.gbk.twm).
  • Bible text. For a Bible, or some books of one, typed in a document: a line with the book name (Genesis), chapter lines (Chapter 2, or Genesis 2), and verse numbers at the start of each verse (3 And God said…). Several verses in one paragraph work when the numbers count up (1, 2, 3). Lines like John 3:16 For God so loved… work too. If Timotheos finds no verses it tells you and nothing is added. It becomes a USFM Bible in the Bible list.
  • Notes in My Study. Each paragraph that starts with a verse reference (John 3:16 …, Rom 8:28-30) becomes your note on that verse, together with the paragraphs after it, up to the next reference. If the verse already has a note, the new text is added below it. Importing the same file twice adds nothing the second time.

Pick Do the same for the other documents in the window when you import several at once. Skip this file leaves a file out.

Limits. Footnotes, headers, text boxes and tracked changes in Word files are not imported. In an .rtf, tables arrive as lines with tab spaces, not as a table (Word, .odt files keep their tables). Pictures that a browser cannot show (.emf, .wmf) are left out, and the result window says how many. Edit a book with pictures or tables carefully: the book editor saves text, lists and emphasis, and may drop tables and pictures of the section you edit. Importing the same title again makes a second copy, Title (2), and never overwrites the first. This was tested on Linux with files from Word-compatible programs (LibreOffice, pandoc, python-docx), not with Microsoft Word itself.

SWORD modules (CrossWire)

The SWORD Project (crosswire.org/sword) publishes hundreds of free Bibles. Timotheos reads Bibles, commentaries, dictionaries and lexicons (Strong's Greek and Hebrew too) and general books, compressed with zlib, bzip2 or xz.

  1. On the CrossWire module page choose a Bible and download its Raw ZIP package (a .zip file). Do not choose the installer or the SWORD folder.
  2. Use File → Import Modules… (Ctrl+I) and choose the .zip (or drag it onto the window). Timotheos unpacks it into its own SWORD folder. You can import several packages at once.
  3. A Bible appears in the Bible list, marked SWORD, with Strong's numbers, morphology, footnotes (f), cross-references (x) and the words of Jesus where the module has them. A commentary, dictionary or general book appears in the Library list, and the Commentary panel shows the commentaries for the verse you are reading. A Strong's dictionary is used by the word pop-up like any other Strong's dictionary.
  • Locked modules (those that need an unlock key from the publisher) are skipped, and the import window says so. Timotheos does not open them.
  • Verse numbering. Some Bibles number their verses differently from the King James Bible (for example the Vulgate or Luther Bible in the Psalms). Timotheos shows every verse under the module's own number. When the numbering differs, a note appears under the module list (Options → Modules) while that Bible is open, and a verse can then sit a number or two away from the same verse in a King James-numbered Bible when you compare them side by side. Timotheos does not move verses between numbering systems.
  • Very old modules with the LZSS compression are not read; the import window tells you.
This was tested with test packages made for Timotheos and not yet with every module on the CrossWire list. If a Bible shows wrong text, send the problem report from Help and name the module.

MySword modules

Modules from MySword (mysword.info) are read directly: Bibles (with Strong's numbers, footnotes and verse rules), commentaries, dictionaries (including Strong's dictionaries), books and journals. They are shown read-only; Timotheos never changes a .mybible file.

  1. Open Options → Modules and click Add folder… (Ctrl+O), then choose the folder that holds your MySword files (for example mysword/bibles), or drag the files onto the window (File → Import Modules…, Ctrl+I) to copy them in.
  2. The modules appear in the Library and the Bible list. A MySword book shows its chapters in the book's own order and with its own styling (raised small verse numbers); entries the module marks as hidden are left out.
MySword's dictionaries, books and journals have no published layout, so Timotheos finds their columns by name. If one of yours shows as unreadable, send the problem report from Help.

Files without .bbl in the name (0.6.68). A file called just Something.mybible is no longer taken for MySword's personal notes: Timotheos looks inside and reads it as a Bible, commentary, dictionary or book, on import and in a module folder. Imported, it is kept as Something.bbl.mybible (or .cmt, .dct, .bok). Only a real notes file is refused.

Module properties

Open a module's properties with ⓘ on the Library bar, or with the small ⓘ that appears when you rest the mouse on a module in the Bibles or Modules panel.

  • About: the module's description shown as a formatted page. Edit lets you change it, with bold, italic, underline and lists. Timotheos saves it in the form the module's program reads: RTF for theWord and e-Sword 9/10, HTML for e-Sword 11.
  • Details: title, abbreviation, description, author, version, publisher and so on.
  • All settings: every other setting stored in the module. Settings that belong to the program that made the module are shown greyed out and are not changed.
  • File & size: where the file is and how big it is.
    • Make smaller compacts the file. This removes unused space; nothing in the module changes.
    • For theWord books you can also compress the text. Timotheos reads compressed books, but it has not yet been checked whether theWord reads books compressed by Timotheos, so test it in theWord first.
  • Verse links (theWord books, dictionaries and commentaries): see Create verse links below.

Before the first change, Timotheos keeps a copy of the original file next to it (…timotheos-backup). Put the original file back undoes all changes. Locked (encrypted) modules cannot be changed.

Create verse links

A book, dictionary or commentary often mentions verses as plain text. Module properties → Verse links finds them and makes them links that open the verse, the way theWord's own links work (tw://bible…), so the module also works in theWord. Links that are already in the module are never changed, except that the placeholder text [vref] is replaced by the real reference.

  1. Open the module's properties (ⓘ) and choose Verse links.
  2. Choose what to link:
    • Tagalog book names (Juan, Mga Awit, Roma …) are known as well as the English names and abbreviations (John, Jn, 1 Cor, I Cor, 1st John, Php …).
    • References that follow a book name: Exo 4:14; 5:1, 8 and 9 become three links. A range such as Rom 8:28-30 or Gen 1:1-2:3 is one link.
    • A bare 3:16 after a book name in the same paragraph. This is off at first because it can also catch times such as 10:30.
    • [vref] links show the real reference.
  3. Choose the link text: as written (kept), or the standard way with English or Tagalog abbreviations.
  4. Choose where: in the module itself (the original file is kept; File & size → Put the original file back undoes it) or in a new copy next to it called … (verse links).
  5. Click Preview to see how many links would be made and some examples; nothing changes. Then Create the links.
Links are made in theWord modules (.twm) only, and not in modules whose text is compressed or holds pictures-and-text entries. A reference across two books is never one link (theWord does not allow it). Please test in theWord a module you changed; Timotheos has only been tested reading these links back.

[vref] in a link. If the text of a link shows [vref] (the AnalyticalConcordance module does), the link points to the right verse but the exporter never wrote the reference. Timotheos shows the real reference for it when it displays the entry, in every module, without changing the file. Verse links can also write the reference into the module itself. theWord shows the text exactly as it is in the file.

To remove an imported module, rest the mouse on it in the Modules panel and click ×. The copy goes to the Recycle Bin. Modules read from your own folders are never deleted.

Timotheos

Getting started

  1. Timotheos comes with one Bible, Ang Malayang Biblia (New Testament), so there is something to read at once. The first time, choose one of three screens; Timotheos then starts in that screen. You can change it later with Change screen (in More, in Help or in the command bar):
    • Quiet Reader: only the text and one floating capsule.
    • Clear Tabs: tabs at the bottom for Bible, Library, Search, Notes and More.
    • Study Desk: the text with drawers on the left and right and a dock at the bottom.
  2. Bring in your modules with Modules → Import: choose theWord or e-Sword files on your phone. Timotheos checks each file, keeps a copy of the ones it can use, and tells you why any file was not imported. .zip and .twzip files are unpacked.
  3. Tap the Bible's short name (for example KJV) to choose another Bible.

Touch

  • Swipe the text left or right: next or previous chapter.
  • Tap a word: a small pop-up with its dictionary entries. Drag it by its top bar to move it. Timotheos remembers where you put it. Throw it off the screen to close it. Dictionary opens the full entry, and Find searches for the word.
  • Hold a verse: its tools (copy, highlight, underline, read aloud, compare, cross-references, commentary, note, bookmark, add to a list and more).
  • Pinch with two fingers: the Bible or book text gets larger or smaller. The buttons and menus keep their size.
  • Popups go back the way they came. Drag a sheet from the bottom back down, a list that dropped from the top bar back up, a page from the right back to the right, and a drawer back to its side. You don't need the ✕. A list inside a popup scrolls first; drag again once it is at its end.
  • Swipe up from the bottom edge: the bottom bar (tab bar, capsule or dock) comes back after it hid while you read. A tap on the text brings it back too. The phone's own home gesture uses the lowest strip of the screen, so start just above it.
  • The phone's Back button or gesture closes the topmost popup, page or drawer first.

Quiet Reader

  • Tap the page to show the top strip: Back, Forward, the place, Search and More. It hides again by itself.
  • Tap the place (for example John 3) to go to a book and chapter. Tap the Bible's short name beside it: the list of your Bibles drops down from under the strip, next to the name.
  • The big title at the top of the chapter works the same way. Its Aa button opens the reading view settings.
  • The capsule at the bottom: previous and next chapter, the reference (tap it for Book → Chapter → Verse) and read aloud. While a chapter is read aloud, it becomes a player.
  • More: a search box for verses, books and commands, then Library, Bible search, Book search, Commentary, My Study, Parallel views, Copy verses, Daily readings, Read aloud, Modules, Options and Help.

Clear Tabs

  • The top bar: Back, Forward, Aa (reading view) and read aloud.
  • Under it: the place (tap it for the list of books and chapters), and the Bible chip (tap it for the list of your Bibles). Two Bibles at once is in Options → Layout → Parallel Bibles and under More.
  • Tap a verse: a small toolbar to copy, highlight, underline or write a note. More adds read aloud, compare, cross-references, commentary, dictionary and bookmark. Hold a verse for all its tools.
  • The tabs: Bible, Library, Search, Notes and More. More holds read aloud, appearance, layout, verse numbers, reading view, modules, copy verses, clipboard monitor, daily readings, fonts, all options, the guide and About.

Study Desk

  • The rail at the top: Back, the reference (it opens the Navigator), the Bible's short name (the list of your Bibles drops from under the rail), Aa and Forward.
  • From the left edge: the Navigator, with books and chapters, history and bookmarks. From the right edge: the study drawer, with commentary, dictionary, cross-references, note and library, following the verse. Drag a drawer back to its edge to close it.
  • Pull down from the top, or tap the small handle under the rail: quick settings. Push them back up to close them.
  • The dock: Books & chapters, the command bar, read aloud and the study drawer. Pull up from the dock: the split desk with Parallel, Commentary, Library and Search.
  • Command bar: type a reference, a book, a word or a command.
  • Hold a verse: a quick row (copy, underline, note, read aloud, three highlight colours, more).

Reading

  • Two Bibles (Parallel): one above the other, with a divider you can drag. Each one has its own bar with its name, its place and its own Aa.
  • Reading view (Aa): the Bible, text size, margins, line height and text alignment (left, centre, justified, right).
    • Show in the text: Strong's numbers, morphology, interlinear, footnotes, headings, words of Jesus, added words, cross-references, commentary marks, your highlights and notes, and the module's own tags (for checking a module).
    • This view: font, heading font, text colour, active verse colour, the highlighter and underline pens, moving with the other view, verse numbers and About this Bible.
  • The text flows in paragraphs. A new line or paragraph starts only where the Bible module puts one.
  • Read aloud reads from the selected verse and goes on into the next chapter. Its settings choose a voice for each language, the speed, and whether to repeat a chapter or a range of verses. The voices are the ones your phone has.
    • It keeps going in the background. A background service reads, so it carries on when the screen is locked or you are in another app. The next verses are lined up ahead, so there are no gaps between them, and the next four chapters are lined up too.
    • Player in the notification and on the lock screen: previous verse, pause, next verse and stop. The first time, Android asks to allow notifications: choose Allow.
    • Battery saver: the first time you read aloud, Android asks whether Timotheos may run without battery restrictions: choose Allow. Later, find the same button in Options → Read aloud → Background playback. Xiaomi/Redmi phones also have their own setting that only you can change: Settings → Apps → Timotheos → Battery saver → No restrictions.
    • Open the app again while it is reading and it shows the player and marks the verse being read.
  • Next chapter: at the end of a chapter, a Next chapter card comes after the footnotes. Tap it to go on (or swipe).
  • Chapter buttons: the capsule (Quiet Reader) and the reading bar have previous and next chapter buttons. If the Bible is New Testament only, the Old Testament books are not offered.
  • The Apocrypha sits between Malachi and Matthew, as in the 1611 King James Bible. Options → Modules → Place the Apocrypha moves it after Revelation.

Library, search and study

  • Library: your commentaries, dictionaries, books, devotionals and maps. Devotionals open at today's reading.
  • Search: the Bibles, or every library book. Some examples:
    • faith hope: both words.
    • "in the beginning": the exact phrase.
    • love*: words that start with love.
    • G26 or H430: a Strong's number.
  • Study: your notes, highlights, bookmarks, verse lists and reading plans. Back up my study saves them all as one file where you choose (for example Downloads or Drive); Restore reads such a file, also one made on a computer.
  • Cross-references show as a small mark such as †3 at the end of a verse; tap it to list them. Aa → Cross-refs written out prints them in the text.
  • Copy verses: pick the range and the format. Ranges can run across chapters and books.
  • Your own marks in books: select words in a commentary or book and choose a colour, bold or underline. The module itself is not changed.

Notes and My Learnings

  1. Hold a verse (or tap it, in Clear Tabs) and tap Note.
  2. Topic: the main subject, up to 4 words. Topics you used before show as chips under the box and as suggestions: tap one to use it.
  3. Subtopic: a short heading, up to 5 words. Tap Add a subtopic (lower right) for up to 4 in one note; the × removes one. Subtopics you used under this topic before are suggested.
  4. Explanation: what you learned from the verse. Then Save. The Topic and at least one Subtopic are required.

Notes with the same topic are gathered across the whole Bible. Open My Learnings from Study → Notes → Open My Learnings or from the Library, among your dictionaries. In Study → Notes switch between By topic and By verse and use the filter box. Export as a dictionary and Export as a commentary save your notes as theWord files where you choose.
